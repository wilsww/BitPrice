### User documentation
No end-user documentation exists. The app ships with no explanation of how to

clicking around. This was listed as a Phase 6 delivery item and has never been

### Untested paths
Both have implementations and neither has an automated test or a desktop walk-
through. They are recorded as untested rather than as defects — the code exists
and reads correctly, which is precisely the state that has already proven
insufficient in this project: seven review rounds and 68 tests passed before six
defects appeared within minutes of the first real desktop launch.

- **Deleting the primary coin, and its fallback.** `AppModel.removeItem`
  reassigns `primaryItemID` to `draft.items.first?.id` when the deleted item was
  primary. Deleting the last remaining item leaves `primaryItemID` nil. Neither
  the reassignment nor the empty-watchlist case has been exercised.
- **Corrupt-file recovery mode.** `PersistenceStore` has several paths into
  `recovery(...)`: unreadable file, unregistered schema version, a version newer
  than this app supports, and failed validation. `recoveryProtected` then blocks
  writes until an explicit reset. Only the migration and validation paths are
  covered by tests; the read-failure and newer-version paths are not, and the
  read-only behaviour has never been seen on a desktop.

### The panel background lets whatever is behind it bleed through
Raised 2026-08-15. Not yet evaluated.

Confirmed on the desktop: with a green-heavy window behind the panel, the middle
of the coin list takes on a visible green cast and the rows there are harder to
read. The tint moves with whatever is behind the panel, so the list's legibility
depends on an application BitPrice has nothing to do with.

The panel never sets a background of its own. `Views.swift` applies backgrounds
only to individual cards and rows (`.secondary.opacity(0.08)` and similar), so
the full-panel surface is whatever `MenuBarExtra(.window)` supplies by default,
which is translucent.

Worth considering: give the panel an explicit opaque or near-opaque background,
or a material chosen for legibility rather than the default. Two things to check
before settling on one:

- The panel must stay correct in both light and dark appearance, and follow a
  change while it is open.
- Fully opaque loses the visual connection to the menu bar that a menu-bar app
  normally has. A thicker material may be the better trade than flat opacity —
  worth comparing side by side rather than picking from the name.

### Quit is too close to where the pointer already is
Raised 2026-08-15. Not yet evaluated.

Opening Settings means clicking the gear at the top-right of the panel. Quit sits
at the top-right of the Settings screen — the same corner the pointer is already
in. A double-click on the gear, or a click that lands just after the screen
swaps, puts the second click on Quit and the app exits.

This is a near-miss by construction, and it is made worse by two earlier
decisions that were each right on their own: Quit has no confirmation step (the
`PanelRoute.confirmQuit` screen was deliberately removed, on the reasoning that
gear → Settings → Quit is already three steps), and Back/Quit were deliberately
placed at opposite ends so a destructive control would not sit next to a safe
one. Neither anticipated that the *entry* point shares a corner with Quit.

Worth considering: move Quit away from the top edge entirely (it is the least
frequent action on the screen and does not need prime position), or reinstate a
confirmation now that the adjacency is known, or both. Note that a confirmation
cannot be an alert or dialog — the panel closes the moment it loses key-window
status.

### Add-coin venue selection is probably unnecessary ceremony
Raised 2026-08-15. Not yet evaluated in depth; recorded before it is forgotten.

**The observation.** Adding a coin makes the user answer two market pickers
before the Add button enables. The two venues are always the same two, and their
roles are already fixed in code: Binance is the live WebSocket source, Coinbase
is the 60-second polled fallback, and `chartSelection` / `reconcileFallbacks`
already switch between them automatically when one is unavailable. So the step
looks like it asks the user to configure something the app has already decided.

**What the step actually asks, which is not the same thing.** The user is not
choosing *which venue*; they are choosing *which market on each venue* —
`BTCUSDT` versus `BTCFDUSD`, `BTC-USD` versus `BTC-USDC`. Those differ in quote
asset, and therefore in price. Removing the pickers outright would mean picking
a market on the user's behalf, which is a different and larger decision than
removing a redundant venue choice.

**Questions to settle before implementing anything.**
- Can a market be chosen automatically with a rule that is right often enough —
  preferring USD, then USDT, then highest liquidity? What happens for a coin
  where the rule picks a thin market?
- `AddCoinView` currently enables Add only "after both venues are answered and at
  least one has a market". If selection becomes automatic, what does the failure
  case look like when neither venue lists the coin at all?
- Should an automatic choice still be *visible* — shown as "Using BTCUSDT" with
  a way to change it — or entirely hidden? The project's recurring defect shape
  is an engine that is correct while the UI discloses nothing (the hysteresis
  and alerts-off defects), and a silently chosen quote asset is that shape.

Related code: `AppModel.loadCandidates`, `AddCoinView` (`binanceCandidates`,
`coinbaseCandidates`, `selectedBinance`, `selectedCoinbase`, `canAdd`),
`VenueCandidate`, `VenueMapping.provenance`.

#### Chart time range evaluation (2026-08-15)

The 24-hour window is hardcoded in three places that are not independent
parameters but three expressions of one decision:

- `AppModel.refreshChart` — `end.addingTimeInterval(-86_400)`
- `RESTClients.binanceCandles` — `interval=1h`, `limit=25` (25 is 24÷1 + 1)
- `RESTClients.coinbaseCandles` — `granularity=3600`

Adding ranges without unifying these would turn one guessed constant into
three, which is the defect shape already removed twice from this project
(`nonChartHeight`, `yAxisWidth`).

**Venue granularity limits.** Binance takes an interval enum
(`1m/3m/5m/15m/30m/1h/2h/4h/6h/8h/12h/1d/...`) with `limit` up to 1000.
Coinbase accepts only `60/300/900/3600/21600/86400` seconds and at most 300
candles per request. The chosen ranges must be expressible on both, since
`chartSelection` falls back between venues.

| Range | Granularity | Candles | Binance | Coinbase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24h | 15m | 96 | `15m` | `900` |
| 7d | 6h | 29 | `6h` | `21600` |
| 30d | 1d | 31 | `1d` | `86400` |

All three are legal on both venues and well under the 300-candle cap, so no
per-venue table and no pagination is needed.

**Decisions.**

- **15m for 24h, not 30m.** 30m was requested, but Coinbase has no 1800-second
  granularity, so the two venues would draw different densities for the same
  range. 15m exists on both, is finer than the current 1h, and stays at 96
  candles.
- **The range is global, not per-coin.** Only one chart is on screen at a time.
- **The range persists.** A new `PersistedState` field with a default,
  `currentSchemaVersion` stays 1 — the same additive pattern used for
  `alertsEnabled`, `textSize`, and the alert defaults.
- **The baseline mismatch is accepted and left alone.** At 7d the chart baseline
  is the 7-day-old close, while the row's percentage comes from the WebSocket
  ticker and is always 24h. Two different bases sit side by side. The ui15
  tooltip already says "this chart series' opening close", which stays accurate.
  Not annotating it is a deliberate choice, recorded here so a later reader does
  not mistake it for an oversight.

**Required plumbing.** `ChartRequestToken` needs the range as a field: without
it, a stale 24h response arriving after a switch to 7d cannot be distinguished
from a current one, and would overwrite the newer series. This is correctness,
not a feature — `marketRevision` already exists for the same class of problem.
`ChartSeries` needs it too, so the X axis can format labels by range.

**Untested area this touches.** `normalizedCandles` de-duplicates by
`bucketStart` used as a dictionary key. Its behaviour at bucket boundaries has
only ever been exercised at 1h granularity.

## Key questions
1. How are watched assets identified and deleted, so selection can fall back safely?
2. How are chart venue/quote disclosure and stale/failure states currently derived?
3. What icon-generation mechanism is available without third-party assets or a GUI?

## Decisions
| Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Keep UI selection local to the panel view | Selection is presentation state and must remain distinct from persisted primary selection. |
| Preserve inline `PanelRoute` editors | Separate presentations close a `.window` MenuBarExtra panel. |
| Use single-selection `List(selection:)` tagged by item UUID | Makes the row selectable without nesting the star and Alert buttons inside another button. |
| Put reorder and direct delete controls in the selected detail area | Removes row clutter while keeping structural controls explicit and inline. |
| Draw PNGs headlessly with Core Graphics, then package them with `iconutil` | Produces an original, reproducible icon without downloads, GUI tools, or third-party assets. |

## Errors
| Error | Attempts | Resolution |
|---|---:|---|
| `rg` is unavailable on this host | 1 | Use `find`, `grep`, and `sed` as the documented fallback. |
| Icon-generator `swiftc` tried to write its default module cache under `~/.cache` | 1 | Give the generator a private module cache inside its temporary work directory. |
| macOS 15.6 `iconutil -c icns` rejects complete valid-size iconsets, including one it extracted from Apple's Dock icon | 2 | Keep the required pack attempt, then fall back to a deterministic ICNS container; validate by decoding it with `iconutil -c iconset`. |
| A diagnostic command containing `rm -rf build/IconDebug.iconset` was rejected by the execution safety layer | 1 | Used a uniquely named `mktemp` debug directory instead; no project data was removed. |
| A combined patch expected a duplicated chart-call line that was only an output-display artifact | 1 | Re-read the exact source range and applied the source/test edits against the actual text. |
